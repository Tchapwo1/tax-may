# Section 24: The Landlord’s Survival Guide (2025/26)

Section 24 of the Finance Act 2015 changed the way individual landlords are taxed on their rental income. No longer can you simply subtract your mortgage interest from your rent before calculating tax. Instead, you are taxed on the **gross profit** and given a 20% tax credit.

For higher-rate taxpayers, this often leads to a massive increase in the effective tax rate—sometimes exceeding 100% of the actual cash profit.

## How it Works in Practice

1. **Gross Revenue**: All your rental income is added to your other income (like your salary).
2. **Allowable Expenses**: You can only subtract maintenance, insurance, and management fees.
3. **Tax Calculation**: You are taxed on this total "Taxable Profit" at your marginal rate (20%, 40%, or 45%).
4. **The Credit**: You then receive a tax reduction equal to 20% of your mortgage interest.

## Strategic Considerations

* **Incorporation**: Moving properties into a Limited Company allows interest to be treated as a full expense.
* **Pension Contributions**: Reducing your "Adjusted Net Income" can sometimes bring you back into the basic rate band.
* **Transfer to Spouse**: If your spouse is a basic rate taxpayer, transferring ownership can reduce the overall tax burden.

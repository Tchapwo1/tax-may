How landlord tax works in 2025–26

Rental income is taxed as income — stacked on top of any other income you receive. This means if you have a PAYE salary, your rental profit is taxed at your marginal rate, often 40%.

Section 24 — the mortgage interest restriction

Since April 2020, individual landlords cannot deduct mortgage interest from rental income. Instead, you receive a basic-rate (20%) tax credit on the interest paid. For higher-rate taxpayers this significantly increases the tax on rental income. The restriction does not apply to companies — it applies only to individual landlords.

MTD for landlords — April 2026

Landlords with total income over £50,000 must use MTD-compatible software and submit quarterly digital returns from April 2026. The threshold drops to £30,000 from April 2027. Failure to comply carries penalties.

Frequently asked questions

Section 24 restricts mortgage interest relief for individual landlords. You can no longer deduct mortgage interest from rental profit. Instead you receive a 20% tax credit on the interest paid. Higher-rate taxpayers lose the additional 20% relief they previously received. The calculator applies this correctly.
Allowable expenses include: letting agent and management fees, buildings and contents insurance, repairs and maintenance (not improvements), ground rent, service charges, accountancy fees, advertising, and utility costs for furnished lettings. You cannot deduct mortgage capital repayments, your own time, or capital expenditure (though capital allowances apply to some items for FHLs).
FHLs that meet HMRC's conditions (available 210 days/year, let 105 days/year, not long-term let) are treated as a trade rather than a property investment. This means: full mortgage interest deduction (Section 24 does not apply), capital allowances on furniture and equipment, potentially lower CGT rates, and NI contributions for pension purposes. Note: The FHL regime was announced for abolition from April 2025 — check HMRC for the latest position.

If you own a rental property jointly with a spouse or civil partner, rental income and expenses are normally split 50/50 by default. Each partner declares their share on their own Self Assessment return. You can elect to split income in different proportions (Form 17) if the beneficial ownership differs from 50/50. This calculator applies a 50% share when joint ownership is selected.

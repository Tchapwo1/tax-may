The £1,000 Trading Allowance Explained

Updated April 2025 5 min read Verified by ATT-qualified accountant

The trading allowance lets you earn up to £1,000 from self-employment or casual income without paying any tax — and without needing to file a Self Assessment return. But there are important limits and exceptions you need to understand.

What is the trading allowance?

The trading allowance is a £1,000 tax-free threshold for self-employed income, introduced in 2017–18. It applies to income from self-employment, casual work, and certain online platform activities. The allowance works in two ways:

  • Full exemption: If your total trading income is £1,000 or less in a tax year, you pay no tax on it and do not need to file a Self Assessment return.
  • Alternative deduction: If your trading income is over £1,000, you can claim the £1,000 allowance instead of itemising your actual expenses — useful if your real expenses are low.
Example: You earn £800 selling handmade goods on Etsy. Your trading income is below £1,000 so you pay no tax and do not need to file a return. If you earn £1,200, you can either claim the £1,000 allowance (taxable profit £200) or deduct your actual expenses.

Who can use it?

The trading allowance is available for income from:

  • Freelance and self-employed work
  • Selling goods online (eBay, Etsy, Vinted, Facebook Marketplace)
  • Gig economy platforms (Deliveroo, Uber Eats, TaskRabbit)
  • Casual or occasional work
  • Renting out equipment or tools

The allowance cannot be used for income from employment, property rental, or income from a connected person or employer.

Trading allowance vs actual expenses

You must choose between claiming the £1,000 allowance and claiming your actual expenses — you cannot use both for the same trade. Use whichever gives you the lower taxable profit.

ScenarioTrading allowanceActual expensesBetter option
Income £5,000 / Expenses £200Profit: £4,000Profit: £4,800Trading allowance
Income £5,000 / Expenses £2,000Profit: £4,000Profit: £3,000Actual expenses
Income £5,000 / Expenses £800Profit: £4,000Profit: £4,200Trading allowance
Note: If you use the trading allowance, you cannot carry forward a loss. Actual expenses may be better if you have significant costs even if they are below £1,000, because losses can be offset against future profits.

When you must file a Self Assessment

Even if your income is below £1,000, you must register for and file a Self Assessment return if:

  • You want to pay voluntary Class 2 NI contributions to protect your State Pension entitlement
  • Your combined income from all sources means you owe tax not collected through PAYE
  • You receive income from a spouse or civil partner's business
  • You want to claim a CIS refund (construction workers)
  • HMRC has issued you a notice to file a return

What changes in 2025–26?

The trading allowance remains at £1,000 for 2025–26. There is currently no legislation to increase it in line with inflation. The government has consulted on expanding the allowance for platform economy workers but no changes have been enacted for this tax year.

Note that from April 2026, platform operators (Uber, Deliveroo, Etsy, Airbnb etc.) are required under DAC7 rules to report seller data to HMRC. This significantly increases HMRC's ability to identify undeclared platform income. If you have been earning platform income without declaring it, now is the time to regularise your position.

Allowable Expenses for Sole Traders — Full List

Updated April 2025 8 min read Verified by ACCA-qualified accountant

Claiming every allowable expense reduces your taxable profit — and therefore your tax bill. Most sole traders under-claim. This guide lists every category HMRC allows, with the rules that apply to each.

The golden rule: An expense is allowable if it is incurred "wholly and exclusively" for the purposes of your trade. Mixed-use expenses (like a phone you use for business and personal calls) can be partially claimed based on the business proportion.

Mileage and travel

If you use your own car, van, or motorbike for business journeys, you can claim the HMRC approved mileage rates instead of actual costs. You cannot claim travel from home to a regular place of work.

VehicleFirst 10,000 milesAbove 10,000 miles
Car or van45p per mile25p per mile
Motorcycle24p per mile24p per mile
Bicycle20p per mile20p per mile

If you use simplified mileage rates, you cannot also claim capital allowances on the vehicle. Keep a mileage log noting the date, destination, purpose, and miles for every business journey. HMRC can and does ask for this evidence.

Home office

If you work from home, you can claim a portion of your household running costs. You have two options:

Simplified flat rate (HMRC approved)

Hours worked from home per monthMonthly flat rate
25 to 50 hours£10
51 to 100 hours£18
101 hours or more£26

Actual costs method

Calculate the business proportion of your home running costs (electricity, heating, broadband, council tax) based on the number of rooms used for business and the time spent working. This requires more record-keeping but may give a larger deduction.

Equipment and tools

Equipment purchased for business use can be claimed through capital allowances. For most small businesses, the Annual Investment Allowance (AIA) allows you to deduct the full cost in the year of purchase — the AIA limit is £1 million for 2025–26. This includes computers, cameras, tools, machinery, and office furniture.

Other common allowable expenses

CategoryWhat's allowedWhat's not allowed

What you cannot deduct

  • Your own drawings or salary (you are not your own employee as a sole trader)
  • Business entertainment — HMRC does not allow deductions for client entertainment
  • Capital expenditure (claimed through capital allowances instead)
  • Fines and penalties
  • Non-business proportion of any mixed-use expense

Making Tax Digital — What Sole Traders Must Do

Updated April 2025 6 min read Verified by ATT-qualified accountant

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax Self Assessment (MTD for ITSA) replaces the annual Self Assessment return with quarterly digital submissions. If your income exceeds the threshold, you must comply or face penalties.

Who must comply and when

Annual income thresholdMandatory from
Over £50,000 (self-employed + rental combined)April 2026
Over £30,000April 2027
Over £20,000April 2028 (proposed)
Important: The threshold applies to your total qualifying income — self-employment plus property rental income combined. If you are a sole trader earning £35,000 and a landlord earning £18,000, your combined income is £53,000 and you must comply from April 2026.

What you must do under MTD

  • Keep digital records of all income and expenses throughout the year using MTD-compatible software
  • Submit quarterly updates to HMRC — by 7 May, 7 August, 7 November, and 7 February
  • Submit an End of Period Statement (EOPS) confirming your annual figures
  • Complete a final declaration (replacing your Self Assessment return) by 31 January

Choosing MTD-compatible software

You must use HMRC-recognised software to keep digital records and make submissions. HMRC maintains an approved software list. Common options for sole traders and landlords include QuickBooks, FreeAgent, Sage, and Xero. Spreadsheets are allowed if connected to a bridging tool that submits digitally.

Our MTD service sets you up with the right software and handles your quarterly submissions, freeing you to focus on your business.

Exemptions

You are exempt from MTD if: your income is below the relevant threshold; you have a recognised disability that prevents digital record-keeping; you have religious objections to internet use; or you live in an area with no reliable internet access. Exemptions must be applied for and approved by HMRC.

CIS Tax Explained — and How to Claim a Refund

Updated April 20255 min read

The Construction Industry Scheme requires contractors to deduct tax from subcontractor payments. Many construction workers pay too much — and are owed a refund they never claim.

How CIS deductions work

If you work as a subcontractor in construction, your contractor deducts CIS tax from your gross payment before you receive it. The standard rate is 20% for registered subcontractors and 30% for unregistered ones. These deductions are paid to HMRC on your behalf as advance payments towards your tax bill.

You are likely owed a refund if your CIS deductions exceed your actual income tax and NI liability for the year. This is common for subcontractors with high expenses or part-year working.

How to claim a CIS refund

You claim a CIS refund through your annual Self Assessment tax return. Your CIS deductions are entered as tax already paid, and if they exceed your total tax liability, HMRC repays the difference — usually within 4–6 weeks of filing.

You must have a CIS deduction statement from each contractor you worked for in the tax year. Contractors are legally required to provide these.

How to File a Self Assessment Return as a Sole Trader

Updated April 202510 min read

A step-by-step walkthrough of the HMRC online Self Assessment process for sole traders. Covers registration, filling in your return, expenses, and paying what you owe.

Step 1: Register for Self Assessment

If you haven't filed before, register at gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment. HMRC will send your Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) by post within 10 working days. You must be registered by 5 October following the end of the tax year — so 5 October 2025 for the 2024–25 return.

Step 2: Gather your documents

  • Total self-employed income for the year (invoices, bank statements, platform reports)
  • All allowable expenses, organised by category
  • CIS deduction statements (if construction)
  • P60 or P45 if you also have PAYE income
  • Bank interest statements
  • Any other income received in the tax year

Step 3: Complete your return online

Log in to your HMRC Personal Tax Account or the HMRC online filing service. Complete the self-employment pages — you'll need to enter your total income, total expenses (or use the trading allowance), and any other income sources. The system calculates your tax automatically.

Step 4: Check and submit

Review your return carefully before submitting. Check the tax calculation makes sense — if the figure looks wrong, go back and check your inputs. Submit before 31 January and pay any tax due on the same date.

Payments on account: If your tax bill exceeds £1,000, HMRC requires advance payments (payments on account) towards the following year. These are 50% of the previous year's bill, due 31 January and 31 July. Many first-time filers are caught out by this.

How the £12,570 Personal Allowance Affects Your UK Take-Home Pay

Updated April 2025 Video Guide Verified by ATT-qualified accountant

Do you know why your first paycheck feels smaller than expected? In the UK, the Personal Allowance is the foundation of your earnings. Currently set at £12,570, this is the specific amount you can earn each year entirely tax-free.

How the Tax-Free Threshold Works

The UK tax system functions like a ladder. You only pay the 20% basic rate on income above your £12,570 base.

For example, on a £36,000 salary:

  • £0 – £12,570: 0% Tax (Tax-Free Zone)
  • £12,571 – £36,000: 20% Tax applied only to this slice.

The "60% Tax Trap" Warning

While the allowance is a benefit, it can be withdrawn. Once your income hits £100,000, you lose £1 of your allowance for every £2 earned. This creates a "hidden" 60% marginal tax rate that significantly reduces the value of pay raises in that bracket.

Maximize Your Earnings

To protect your allowance, consider pension contributions. Lowering your "adjusted net income" back below £100,000 can restore your tax-free threshold and preserve benefits like free childcare.

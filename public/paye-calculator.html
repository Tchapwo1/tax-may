How PAYE tax is calculated in 2025–26

PAYE (Pay As You Earn) means your employer deducts income tax and National Insurance directly from your salary each pay period, before you receive it. The amount deducted depends on your annual salary, your tax code, and any adjustments such as pension contributions or student loan repayments.

Frequently asked questions

PAYE income tax is calculated by subtracting your Personal Allowance (£12,570 for 2025–26) from your annual salary, then applying rates of 20% on the first £37,700, 40% on the next portion up to £125,140, and 45% above that. Your employer deducts this monthly via payroll using your tax code.
Your tax code tells your employer how much Personal Allowance to apply. The most common code is 1257L, meaning a £12,570 allowance. L means standard allowance; M or N indicates Marriage Allowance transfer; K means you have untaxed income that exceeds your allowance; BR means all income is taxed at basic rate (often a second job); D0 or D1 means all income taxed at 40% or 45%.
Only the portion of your income above each threshold is taxed at the higher rate — this is called marginal taxation. If your salary rises from £48,000 to £55,000, only £4,730 (the amount above £50,270) is taxed at 40%, not your entire salary. You will always be better off with a pay rise, even if it crosses a threshold.
Salary sacrifice pension contributions reduce your gross salary for tax purposes. This means you pay less income tax and less National Insurance. For a higher-rate taxpayer, every £100 contributed to a pension effectively costs only £60 after tax relief. Enter your pension percentage in the calculator to see the exact saving.
Most PAYE employees do not need to file a Self Assessment. However, you must file if: your income exceeds £100,000; you receive the High Income Child Benefit Charge; you have untaxed income from savings, investments, or property; you are self-employed; or you receive income from abroad.
